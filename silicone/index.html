Why silicone.

NANOROOF is built on a chemistry trusted for decades on commercial roofs, solar panels and power lines. Here’s why it works, what kept it off homes, and what we changed to bring it to asphalt shingles.

The breakthroughThe SPS System
Illustration

Eighty years of silicone

A material that started as a lab curiosity became one of the most trusted in building and industry.

  1. 1904Named

    English chemist Frederic Stanley Kipping coins the term “silicone” while studying silicon compounds.

  2. 1930sDeveloped

    J. Franklin Hyde at Corning Glass Works sees industrial uses in Kipping’s “sticky messes” and begins making them.

  3. 1940Scaled

    Eugene Rochow at General Electric invents the direct process, still the basis of how silicones are made.

  4. 1943Commercialized

    Corning and Dow Chemical form Dow Corning, and the commercial silicone industry begins.

  5. SinceTrusted outdoors

    Silicone becomes a standard for restoring commercial roofs and protecting solar panels and power-line insulators from weather.

  6. NowOn shingles

    NANOROOF brings silicone to residential asphalt shingle roofs.

What silicone does on a roof

Repels waterIts low surface energy makes water bead up and run off instead of soaking in.
Stays flexibleIt has the lowest glass-transition temperature of any polymer: it stays rubbery through winter cold and summer heat.

What kept silicone off homes

Silicone earned its reputation on flat commercial roofs. Three things kept it there.

1Solvent carriers
2Hard to recoat

Cured silicone resists almost everything, including new coatings. Roofing makers say plainly that little sticks to silicone except silicone, and recoats often need adhesion tests and primers.

3Built for low-slope roofs

Commercial silicones are made to form a thick film over low-slope roofs, not to penetrate and bond inside a granulated asphalt shingle on a steep roof.

The breakthrough

Silicone, rebuilt for shingles

The NANOROOF SPS System keeps what makes silicone last and removes what kept it off homes.

Water-based
Recoatable

Formulated to bond to itself, so a roof can be re-treated at the next maintenance cycle without stripping the first coat.

Made for shingles

Penetrates and bonds inside the asphalt, then encapsulates the granules, instead of sitting on top as a surface film.

Measurable

Leaves a bonded layer that can be checked at every visit and recorded in the Roof Record.
